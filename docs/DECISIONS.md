# DECISIONS

The settled design and technology choices for the Grupo Sazón screening agent, D1 to D95. Entries are grouped by topic. Each states what is true **now**; entries about the same thing are merged under one heading that lists every number it covers (code and tests cite those numbers), and a decision that was replaced is named only where it explains the current one. Before acting against an entry, say so and confirm with the user. Add to the entry of its topic when a decision is made.

The full log, with every option considered and its pros and cons, is in git: `git show 4706568~1:DECISIONS.md` (it needs the merge commits of the pull requests; a squash merge would lose it). All entries were decided by the user (2026-10-25 to 2026-10-28) unless listed in section 8 as an agent detail still to confirm.

## 1. The conversation

- **D5, D6, D27: flow.** The candidate may answer several things in one message; the agent asks only what is missing, in a friendly order (name, experience, availability, schedule, start date, city, license). A disqualifier that is already known (no license, outside the area) ends the chat at once, which protects recruiter time.
- **D40, D54: confirmations.** A "no license" or out-of-area answer first gets one confirmation question and only a confirming reply ends the chat (the real model read "no tengo coche" as "no license"; one misread must never reject anyone). A changed answer is confirmed ("Antes me dijiste X. ¿Lo cambio a Y?") and changed only on a yes, read in plain code.
- **D16, D17, D28, D41, D19, D26, D67, D68: answers and field rules.** An unclear answer is asked again with an explanation. After 5 follow-ups the agent says it cannot continue until the field is given, and repeats that: outcome `BLOCKED` (reason `BLOCKED_ON_<FIELD>`), cleared when the field is given; model failures never count (D39). Experience: years and platform are recorded and never disqualify; zero years needs the candidate to say they have none (`no_experience`), a plain "yes" is not a number; known platforms are recognised by common spellings, unknown ones are stored as "other" (no free text). Name: two or more words of letters, hyphens, apostrophes. Start date more than 12 weeks away is flagged, never rejected. Availability: "N days a week" below a full job is part-time; weekends needs weekends, Saturday or Sunday.
- **D81 (schedule): no schedule question after full time.** Full time means the whole day, so such a candidate is never asked "morning, afternoon or evening": the schedule is saved as flexible, marked `implied` in the saved sources, and the card shows flexible. It is the engine's general rule (`FieldSpec.implied`): an answer the candidate gives replaces it with no change question, and changing the answer it followed (full time to part time) drops it, so it is asked. Part time and weekends are still asked.
- **D55, D14, D50: off-topic, a person, pay.** Off-topic gets a fixed redirect and the same question; the third off-topic message in total (not in a row; hesitation like "mmm" does not count) ends the chat with a farewell about the messages, never the person, final until a recruiter acts. Asking for a person or about pay is not a strike: a fixed sentence says a recruiter reviews and will answer, then the next question; an event is logged. The agent never hands over to a person itself.
- **D29, D56, D10, D35, D38, D58: stopping, silence, coming back.** Stop wording ("stop", "para", "me retiro") is read in plain code and closes the chat as `WITHDRAWN`. Silence: one reminder after 24 hours, `STALLED` after 24 more, any reply resets it; a reminder job (`python -m sazon.reminders`) queues the messages in an outbox for a real channel, and also covers qualified candidates who ignore the offer of times. The browser chat's "wait a day" button runs that job's own step for one candidate (`reminders.wait_a_day`: the wait skipped, the real time stamped, the job's fixed texts and its consent rule, so someone who has not answered the consent question is not contacted). While applications are open a candidate can come back: after silence or being blocked, after withdrawing ("continuar") or after declining consent (a later "sí"); it stays closed after qualifying, after a rejection by the agent and after the off-topic exit. A dashboard switch closes applications for everyone.
- **D9, D52: disqualified path.** A polite close with a reason code ("I can't continue with the automated process; a recruiter can review your case"). Every such candidate is in the dashboard's review queue until a recruiter sets a status, so a human can overrule.
- **D11, D67: language.** The chat mirrors the candidate's language, decided in code from the words written (`screener/language.py`): a name, a place or a number changes nothing; the model's own guess is only used when no known word can check a long message.
- **D13: service areas.** A sample list per country (Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia; Ciudad de México, Guadalajara, Monterrey; with nearby towns) in one place (`sazon/areas.py`, which the calendar also reads); the client's real list is still needed.

## 2. Call times and booking

- **D7, D8, D36, D48, D81, D83, D90: offering and booking a call.** A qualified candidate is told "your profile fits, book a call to talk with a recruiter" and offered free times of their own country, in that country's clock. Nothing is promised that no one enforces (no 48-hour promise). With no free time, or after "none", or after going quiet, the candidate is told to write whenever they want to book and is shown the times again each time they write. A booking is atomic and one per candidate; a recruiter's cancellation queues a message. *The browser chat and the service's picker* (D83, D90): the candidate sees a button, then a month where only days with free times can be clicked, then that day's 15-minute times; choosing one books it by the same engine path as typing a number (`turn.handle_slot`, one function `slot_offers.take` for both). These channels start their candidates with `buttons` (saved in their state) and every offer, re-offer, "time taken" and reminder says to use the button, with no numbered list (`offer_button` texts); the service has `GET .../times?month=` and `?day=`, `POST .../book`. *Text channels* (terminal, any other) keep the numbered list and its footer, and a client that gives no button texts keeps the list whatever the channel. Typing a number still books for a candidate who has the button.
- **D82, D87, D86: where times come from, and the two rules about time.** Every recruiter has a weekly pattern, by default Monday to Friday 9:00 to 17:00 in their own country's clock (client setting), plus days off and one-off extra times; calls are 15 minutes (client setting). Free times are rows of the `slots` table made from these patterns when a recruiter is created or changes their week, and once a day for the day that has come, so the one atomic booking code, the offers, the funnel and the calendar are shared and capacity grows with every recruiter (two recruiters free at 10:00 make one option with capacity two). *Booking* gives the call to the recruiter with the fewest calls that day among those free then (then by email); the row offered stands in for its time. A recruiter takes one call at a time (unique index), a recruiter's country is the country of their times, a time inside a clock-change gap is never made, and past free times are deleted each day. *Candidates* book from tomorrow up to four weeks ahead by the call's own country's calendar day, never the same day (`SlotCalendar.candidate_may_book`; a client sets the first day with `book_from_days`). *Recruiters* may book, add or move to any time that has not started (`recruiter_may_book`); that is also what the calendar lists, and a free time of today or beyond four weeks shows as "free, only a recruiter can book it". A time added by hand belongs to a real recruiter in that recruiter's own country (a recruiter adds for themselves, the hiring manager picks one). A recruiter's country cannot be changed after creation.
- **D93 (booking by voice): which day, then which time.** A spoken call has no list and no button, so after "your profile fits" the agent asks which day. A day with free times goes on to "what time?"; a day without (a weekend, today, a full day) gets the closest day with times (two equally close: the earlier) and a yes or no; a time that is free is booked at once, one that is not gets the closest free time of that day (equally close: the earlier) and a yes or no; a part of the day ("in the afternoon") gets its earliest free time. Spanish and English, read in plain code (`screener/spoken_when.py`; with no am or pm, 1 to 7 are the afternoon), nothing sent to the model. Three answers it cannot read, or a decline ("none"), end it with "write to us whenever you want to book". The channel is per message: the same candidate can write the next one and gets the usual offer, and speaking again asks the day again. The call is said with full words ("el jueves 29 de octubre a las 9:15"). A client that gives no `voice` texts keeps the numbered list. Voice never offers the CV upload. A failure on a call (the model too slow, an error inside, an unknown session) is said as "I couldn't hear you. Could you repeat please?" ("No te he oído bien. ¿Puedes repetirlo, por favor?") in the language of the call, not as a technical problem; the written chat keeps its own text. On a call the model gets 5 seconds and no retries, because ElevenLabs gives up on a slow turn.
- **D87, D92 (who): only a candidate whose Estado is Qualified or Interview scheduled is given a call or times.** Qualified by the agent or by a recruiter, and not hired or rejected: `recruiter_rules.can_schedule` (it follows the Estado shown, so a recruiter who qualifies someone the agent turned down can organise their call), asked in every way in through `slot_offers.may_choose_time` (the calendar, refused with `not_schedulable`; a typed number; the picker; the picker's list of times, empty; the reminder job; the chat page). A closed candidate who writes gets the short "this conversation is closed, a recruiter will review your case", never times or "your profile fits"; a released call is not announced to them.
- **D79, D84, D86: recruiter calendar and changes.** The call-times page is a week grid per country (free, booked with the candidate's name, past), with "organise a call" below it for the candidates a call can be organised for (a qualified candidate who ignored the offer is the main case). Booking or moving is one atomic step (`move_booking`): the old time is freed, the new one booked, one audit line by `recruiter:EMAIL`, then the candidate gets a fixed text in their language and their open offer is closed so a late "2" gets the booked time. A day off, a new week or a recruiter leaving: each booked call that no longer fits goes to another recruiter free then (the candidate hears nothing) or is released, with a fixed text and their free times back. Each workflow is one engine call in `recruiter_calls` (`remove_recruiter`, `save_week`, `reset_week`, `set_day_off`, `schedule_new_time`); leaving closes the account first, then tells each released candidate before cancelling their call, so a failure part-way is finished by running it again (at worst telling someone twice). Not one database transaction. *Declined:* a third-party calendar component, overruling the agent to call a disqualified candidate, a second interview booking.
- **Measured (D82, D75).** Thirty candidates choosing the same first time with three recruiters: everyone booked, nobody twice, the load level. Booking load test (`pytest -m load`, a real server, 13 recruiters): 200 candidates over 150 s, p95 5.5 s, no errors; all 200 at once, 217 requests told to retry, p95 23 s; in both, every qualified candidate ended with a call and no recruiter was booked twice. The calendar reads only a week's booked calls and the free count per day.

## 3. What the agent says

- **D22, D45: the engine.** A pure-Python state machine (`Screening`) owns the fields, the decision and every message rule; the model only extracts fields and words messages. Rules are unit-testable offline and candidate text can never change a decision.
- **D31, D39: extraction.** One JSON call per message, validated by a Pydantic model whose shape and prompt come from the client's spec; candidate text is passed as delimited data. Unparseable output is retried once; a failure shows a fixed apology, is logged without candidate data and never costs the candidate a follow-up.
- **D15, D18, D30, D34, D37, D69, D72: wording and persona.** The client writes a persona (tone, length, form of address; Spanish uses "tú"; the agent is called Sofía, who says plainly she is an AI). The model words the questions, follow-ups, place confirmations, change questions, rejection confirmations and the "cannot continue" message, one or two short sentences, given the recent conversation (only after consent, never on the turn that answers the consent question) as data; a model that obeys an injection is not believed. Every model message is checked (length, one question, language, no pay, job or time, and it must keep the facts it was given); a failed check or an unreachable model sends the fixed text. **Fixed word for word:** the AI disclosure and consent, every closing, the call offers and bookings, the reminders, the error line and all notices (a person, pay, change made, answer kept, off-topic).
- **D44: consent is read in plain code.** A conservative Spanish and English word list: short clear agreement is yes, a short explicit refusal closes the chat, anything else asks again. Nothing the candidate writes reaches the model before a yes.
- **D46, D66: the recruiter card.** Built by code from the validated data, never repeating the candidate's words or saying anything about the person. Saved in Spanish with every turn (the audit snapshot) and rebuilt for the screen in the language the recruiter picked.

## 4. Privacy, consent and oversight

- **D12, D34, D51, D72: disclosure and consent.** The first message is fixed, Spanish line then English line: it names Sofía, says the candidate is talking to an AI, that answers are processed by an external AI service and always reviewed by a recruiter, and asks consent to store them. Nothing is asked before a yes. The consent event stores a hash of the exact wording shown (earlier records point to earlier wording). Still a draft until the client's lawyers approve it.
- **D42, D92: recruiter data is separate; the Estado and who is assigned.** The agent owns outcome, reasons, flags, validated data, card and transcript. Recruiters own what they decide (a state and append-only notes) and may set the state of any candidate, so a human can overrule the agent. Every action is an audit event with its actor. **D92, the "Estado" of a candidate:** one word, shown in the table, the filter and the detail: *Contacted* (the conversation is going, also while stalled, blocked or withdrawn; the Agent column says which), *Qualified* (the agent qualified), *Interview scheduled* (the candidate booked a call), *Hired* and *Rejected*. The system moves the first three and Rejected-by-the-agent (disqualified or ended off topic) by itself (`model.stage_of`); a recruiter or the manager can set any of the five at any time, normally Hired or Rejected after the interview, and what they set wins. Choosing the state the system already shows stores nothing (`Store.set_stage`), so it keeps moving: a booked call moves it forward, a released call moves it back; a recruiter's Qualified also becomes Interview scheduled when the call is booked. *New* (nobody decided) is not shown, and the old *Unreachable* is shown as Rejected and no longer offered. **Assigned to** is not typed: it is the recruiter who holds the candidate's booked call, shown by name, empty until they book (the manual assign form is gone). The recruiter card no longer ends with an "automatic summary, the recruiter decides" line.
- **D53, D60: erasure and retention (known gap, section 9).** Every conversation and its data are saved and kept. The dashboard's erase action (with confirmation) overwrites a candidate's data, truncates the database's write-ahead log and leaves an anonymous line; it is for a candidate's request. There is no automatic deletion; how long to keep the rest is for the client's lawyers.
- **D71: optional CV upload (overturns the D20/D58 advice, at the user's request).** After consent the agent offers once an optional PDF upload with the first question; typing an answer is the skip. The browser chat has an uploader with a fixed notice (what is kept, who sees it, that it can be erased; uploading counts as agreeing), the terminal has `/cv <path>`. A PDF is checked by its first bytes and capped at 2 MB, 5 pages, 6000 characters; a scan, an encrypted or a broken file is kept for the recruiter and the questions go on. The model reads the text as data in its own tag with its own prompt, naming only the fields the client allows from a CV (name, city, license, experience; never availability, schedule or start date); anything it says about the conversation is thrown away by code. A value counts when the client's own check accepts it; clear values are taken without asking, then read back so the candidate can correct them (through the usual "change it?" question); a value that would end the screening or a near-miss place waits for the usual confirmations, one at a time. The text is discarded after reading; the PDF is kept in the same transaction as the turn, erased with the candidate, and offered to recruiters as a download, never drawn on the page. The card lists which answers came from the CV; events record size, a fingerprint and the fields, never the content, and the hash of the notice shown. The CV never decides anything. **A real channel must show the notice before it accepts a file.**
- **D93 (voice): audio goes to ElevenLabs as a processor.** A spoken call is heard and transcribed by ElevenLabs; we keep no audio, ask them to keep none (`record_voice` false) and the history one day, never evaluate it or collect data from it, and never infer emotion from the voice. Our own record is the text of the conversation, as in the chat. Zero-retention mode is an Enterprise feature of theirs. The spoken disclosure is the same fixed text, said first and in full.
- **D33, D43: free tier and errors.** Free Gemini prompts may be used by Google to improve its products: fictional candidates only until a paid or EU-compliant tier is chosen. An unexpected error never ends the chat or shows technical text; it goes with a stack trace (never the candidate's messages) to `data/sazon.log` with an id.

## 5. Recruiters and the apps

- **D57, D65, D74, D80, D89: recruiter accounts.** Recruiters live in the database (a booking must check in one atomic step that the recruiter exists, is in the right country, is available and not booked, which a file cannot take part in). Sign-in is by email and password; there is no sign-up. The hiring manager's email and password come from `.env` (`HIRING_MANAGER_EMAIL`, `HIRING_MANAGER_PASSWORD`, never stored, compared in constant time) and only that person creates and deletes recruiters (email, name, country, first password) on the Recruiters page, which is not even listed for anyone else, and can reset a forgotten password. A recruiter changes their own password in the sidebar (the old one is needed). Passwords are salted scrypt hashes; five wrong passwords lock an email (the manager's too) for ten minutes, kept in the database so a restart does not clear it. The email is the one key for a person: audit lines read `recruiter:EMAIL`. **The manager takes calls too (D89):** a recruiter row (name "Hiring manager", the email from `.env`) made when the dashboard or the chat starts, in the country of `HIRING_MANAGER_COUNTRY` (ES or MX, Spain if unset or invalid), with the default week, changeable on the Calendar page like anyone's; its stored password is thrown away so they sign in only with the `.env` one, and the Recruiters page lists them but never offers to delete them or reset their password. This replaces the file accounts of D57/D65 and the open sign-up of D74. A prototype: the dashboard must stay local until single sign-on replaces it.
- **D4, D47, D66, D76, D77: the dashboard.** A local Streamlit dashboard (`apps/dashboard.py`), every word in a Spanish/English text pack with a switch. The candidates page is one column: filters on top, the table full width below (as tall as its rows up to 10, then it scrolls; 50-row pages), then the chosen candidate's details (the agent's summary, data and call beside the recruiter's status, assignee, notes and erasure; the conversation and audit below); a list under the table is the keyboard alternative to clicking a row. It checks the database's change counter every 5 s: with no candidate open it reloads itself, with one open (a note may be half written) it only offers "refresh the list" (`refresh_action`, tested; a timer cannot be run in a test, so check it once in a browser). Both apps share one theme in `.streamlit/config.toml` (warm, one brand colour, no web fonts, so a candidate's browser contacts no font provider) and have a button to each other (`SAZON_CHAT_URL`, `SAZON_DASHBOARD_URL`). At 5,000 fictional candidates (threshold set before measuring: a rerun under 1 s) a rerun takes about 0.3 s (`tests/load/test_dashboard_volume.py`); the one problem was the pick-a-candidate list holding all 5,000, now one page plus the candidate a link names. Known limits: Streamlit's uploader text follows the browser's language, and a `?candidate=` link does not survive the login page. The Phase 1 document is cut to two pages (A4 at 10 pt).
- **D78, D92: the funnel and a candidate's path.** One pipeline from application to hire: started, consent, each answer, qualified, call booked (only for a client that books calls), then the recruiter's one step, hired (`screener/pipeline.py`, pure code, also run through the second client). The call step is labelled "Interview scheduled" (D92): it is the booked call. The recruiter's step, the five states (`STAGES`) and the closed-by-a-recruiter set are written once in `screener/model.py` (`RECRUITER_STEPS`, `STAGES`, `CLOSED_BY_RECRUITER`, `reached_by`, `stage_of`) and read by the pipeline, the funnel's SQL, the table and the recruiter rules. Hired is counted from the audit history (hired, then rejected, still counts as hired). Every candidate has one of four marks: **in process**, **paused** (stalled or blocked), **out** (closed by the agent, withdrawn, off topic, rejected, unreachable) and **hired**; what a recruiter decided overrules the agent's outcome (D42, a recruiter who sets Contacted, Qualified or Interview scheduled keeps a candidate in the process), and a withdrawn candidate counts as out though "continuar" brings them back (the mark is read live). "Went through the whole process" is the number hired. Below the funnel a search and a short list pick one candidate (`pipeline.path_for`) and show each step as done, now, stopped here, a recruiter took over, or still ahead, with the reason for a candidate who is out.
- **D93: the live voice agent (decided with the user, 2026-10-29).** *Wiring:* ElevenLabs does the listening, the speaking and the turn-taking, and for every turn calls our own endpoint (`screener/voice_service.py`, an OpenAI-style chat completions call, streamed); what the engine returns is what is spoken, so the disclosure, the rules and the booking are the same word for word and no second AI decides what to say. The endpoint is a separate small app on its own port (`python -m sazon.voice_server`), the only thing opened to the outside: it checks a shared secret, takes only the last thing the candidate said (never the history ElevenLabs sends), finds the candidate from a short-lived voice session made by our own page (30 minutes, only its hash stored, erased with the candidate; read in one function, `session_of`, from the prompt, where ElevenLabs writes the `voice_session` dynamic variable; extra body is also read, but the agent refuses a call that sends one, as the first live try showed), runs each turn once (session and turn number), answers at once and runs the turn while the first chunk is on its way, ends the call when there is nothing more to say (the call is booked, a qualified candidate took none of the times, or the agent turned the candidate down, they withdrew, were ended for off-topic messages or declined consent: `turn.conversation_is_over`, read from what is saved) by answering with the `end_call` tool call after the farewell, only when the request lists that tool (the agent has it as a built-in tool), and speaks the fixed apology on any failure; with `VOICE_LOG_SHAPES=1` it logs the shape of each call (keys, roles, where the session was, never the words) to `data/voice_requests.log`, to learn from the first real call. *Agent:* made by `python -m sazon.voice_launch` (`screener/voice_agent.py`, plain REST): private (`enable_auth`; only a signed address our server asks for can start a call), its first words set for each call (`{{opening}}`, from `turn.voice_opening`: the exact AI disclosure for someone who has not agreed yet, the way back to the open question for someone midway, the day question for someone qualified, their call if booked, the closing if closed; saved as the agent's message, never the consent question twice) and not interruptible, the call's language set from the candidate's (the one override allowed), the service areas and nearby towns given as recognition hints, no audio recorded (`record_voice` false), history kept one day (`retention_days` 1), no analysis or data collection, a 15-minute cap; its secret and id are in `data/voice.json` (git-ignored, no API key). A tunnel (`cloudflared`, no account) gives ElevenLabs a public address to the endpoint only; the address changes every time, so the launcher moves the agent to it. *Page:* a "Talk to Sofía" button in the chat (off, with how to set it up, until a key and an agent exist) starts a call for the current candidate; the call is saved as the same messages, so it shows in the dashboard, and the transcript redraws on its own while the call page stays where it is; ending it or starting another candidate removes the call. A call that ends by itself or by the candidate is closed on the page too (the call page presses the page's own end button; an error is left on screen to be read), and if the page could not be reached it closes the call itself 30 seconds after the conversation is over; the button is then off and says \"Llamada ya realizada\" (or \"Conversación terminada\" when it ended by text), and a late press starts nothing. *Free tier:* a spoken turn makes one model call (reading the answer), not two, with the fixed words and not the model's wording, because speech is several turns a minute and the free model allows 15 calls a minute; it also shortens the silence before Sofía answers. *Declined:* a browser relay through the agent's own AI (it could reword the fixed texts), a record-and-send page (not a conversation), and storing audio.
- **D1, D66, D73, D91: the browser chat.** Terminal chat first (`python -m sazon.chat`); the browser chat (`apps/chat_app.py`) is for trying the agent on your own computer: it has no login and a live inspector of what the agent understood. It always saves to the recruiters' database, so every candidate it starts shows in the dashboard at once and the times offered are the real recruiters' (at least the hiring manager's, which it makes sure of when it starts); there is no switch and no database of its own. The page says that what is written is saved in the dashboard and should be erased from there. `python demo_launch.py` starts the chat (8501) and the dashboard (8502) together, checks the ports first and stops both on Ctrl+C. *Consequence:* test conversations mix with real candidates in a real deployment, so a real channel goes through the service.

## 6. Technology

- **D2, D23, D33: model.** Gemini free tier through the OpenAI SDK against Gemini's compatible endpoint, a thin client behind an interface so tests use a fake. Model name from `LLM_MODEL` in `.env`.
- **D3: storage.** SQLite (versioned saves under a write lock, a change counter for the dashboard cache).
- **D75: HTTP service and load test.** `screener/service.py` is a FastAPI app factory (engine, no client named); `sazon/server.py` plugs Grupo Sazón in (`python -m sazon.server`; refuses to start without `SAZON_API_KEY`). Calls: `POST /candidates` (id, a secret token, the disclosure), `POST /candidates/{id}/messages` (`message_id`, `text`), `POST /candidates/{id}/cv` (multipart), `GET .../outbox` and `POST .../outbox/ack` (the channel confirms what it sent), `GET /health`, plus the picker calls (section 2). *Who:* a candidate's token opens only that candidate (only its SHA-256 is stored, erased with the candidate); the adapter's API key acts for any and is needed to start one when set; a wrong token and an unknown id both answer 404. *One answer per message id:* the id is claimed before any work; a resend gets the stored reply (no write lock, no model call, no second save); a simultaneous copy waits for the first (409 after 20 s); a failed message releases its id; if the reply cannot be recorded the claim stays so a resend never runs it again; a claim nobody finished is taken over after 5 minutes. *Rate limit,* counted from the stored messages so it holds across workers and restarts: 20 a minute and 300 in total per candidate, 429 before the model call and any save. *Uploads over 2 MB* get 413 before anyone parses the body. *Overload:* when the single writer cannot be reached in 10 s the answer is 503 with `Retry-After` (resending the same id is safe); a candidate whose token could not be saved is erased again. Unexpected errors are logged with an id and answer only `{error, error_id}`. Handlers are plain functions in a pool of 200 threads (async declined: the model's rate limit and SQLite's single writer are the limits, and no lock is held while waiting for the model). *Load test* (`pytest -m load`, a real uvicorn server, a fake model sleeping 1 to 3 s and failing 3%, messages sent twice at once): assumed peak 1000 applications a week, a fifth in the busiest 2 hours, about 10 messages each, about 0.3 messages a second. 200 candidates over 150 s (about 25 times the peak): p95 5.5 s, no busy answers, no errors; all 200 at once: 99 told to retry, p95 19 s, one request waited 5 minutes; every outcome right, no call time given twice, every answered message applied once. **Measured limit:** SQLite commits about 65 writes a second and each message makes about 3, so the service asks callers to retry beyond about 10 messages a second (about 35 times the peak); Postgres, or `synchronous=NORMAL` (about 35% faster, a power cut may lose the last commits), is the next step. The real model was not load-tested (free tier: 15 requests a minute).
- **D95: the demo database and how a call fails.** `python -m sazon.demo.seed` fills an empty database with 27 fictional candidates, four recruiters (password in the command's output), calls, hiring statuses, notes and one erasure, through the real engine with scripted answers: no model, no network, the same result each time (`--reset` deletes the database first; a database that has candidates is refused, never mixed). On a call the model gets 5 seconds and no retries (ElevenLabs gives up on a slow turn), and any failure is spoken as "I couldn't hear you. Could you repeat please?", in the call's language; the written chat keeps "technical problem".
- **D85: how the tests are kept small.** A table of inputs and expected outputs for a pure function or rule is one test, written with `tests/support/tables.table` (like `pytest.mark.parametrize`, but every row runs in one test and the failing rows are listed with their reasons). A table whose rows need a fresh database, app or candidate stays as separate cases; tests that start from the same page and only check one thing each were merged into one scenario. The default run went from 1,534 tests to about 1,000 with the same checks (about 1,050 now); a cheap password hash is used in tests.

## 7. Code structure and reviews

- **D62, D63, D64, D65: engine and client.** `screener/` is the reusable engine and never imports or names a client; `sazon/` is Grupo Sazón. A client is a `ScreeningSpec` (fields, rules, wording, recruiter labels, persona), a `Pack` (fixed texts), a `Client` (service areas, optional call calendar, reminder clock) and its entry points; an incomplete spec or client fails when built. `tests/support/toy_spec.py` is a second client run through the real engine; `tests/engine/test_engine_boundary.py` fails if the engine leaks; saved states, prompts, fixed texts and the consent hash are pinned by a golden test. Screening state is a declared list of saved fields and a pure change-proposal function; the precedence of "what to say first" is a pure function, while the order of checks inside `apply` lives in `Screening`. A `Store` interface was declined (one adapter). Funnel and key numbers are counted in SQL, every stored time is naive local time.
- **D70, D88: architecture reviews (2026-10-27 and 2026-10-28), every candidate built.** *Second review (D70):* `screener/conversation.py` is the one save cycle (load, change, save, and a policy on a lost race: raise, retry once, skip); `screener/language.py` holds accent folding, language detection and the pay, hire and time words (the yes/no/stop/pick word lists stay in `consent.py` and `slot_choice.py`; a third language still edits those); `screener/recruiter_rules.py` defines NEEDS_REVIEW, TO_CONTACT, BLOCKED, STALLED and AGENT_CLOSED once, as Python and SQL, with a test that they agree, and `RecruiterView(client, pack, language)` gives every screen its labels; `screener/directive.py` holds Directive and Kind; the engine says "area", not "city" (wire names `pending_city`, `out_of_area`, `confirms_city` stay); a table test of the order in which one message is processed; `screener/dialog.py` shares starting a candidate and delivering queued messages. *Third review (D88):* `Store` is made of parts: `Database` (the file, the connection, the client's calendar), `CallTimes` (recruiters and sign-in, weekly hours, the times and bookings: one module because one booking needs all of it in one atomic step), `ServiceState` (tokens and replies) and `Store` itself (candidates, recruiter actions, settings, reads); `Store` keeps every method name, forwarded to its part, so no caller changed. Who may be booked and which times count are the engine's (section 2); the recruiter workflows are one call each; the chat button and the reminder job share a path; one source for the service areas with their country, one `bi()` two-language helper and one `hire` test helper. *Declined:* splitting `Screening`'s apply steps into another module and splitting reports out of `Store` (D70); inlining `screening_precedence` (one pure rule with its own table test), `sazon/recruiter_view.py` (the client's binding of the engine's view) and `ui.t` (224 call sites), and making the 48 engine test files stop importing the Sazón client (a large rewrite for little gain; the boundary test passes and the toy client already covers the engine alone) (D88).
- **D94: fourth architecture review (2026-10-29), seven candidates built, one commit each.** *The channel* (`screener/channel.py`): text, button and voice are small settings values (how times are offered, whether the model words the reply, whether a file can be sent, how a time is said) and `effective(channel, client)` is the one fallback when a client lacks the spoken or button texts; the saved `buttons` key is unchanged and reminders and cancelled-call notices use the candidate's usual channel, never voice. *Who may be given a call* (`recruiter_rules.may_schedule`, `may_choose`): a yes or no with a reason (unknown, not qualified, closed, no calendar, already booked), pure over a record. *`slot_flow.py` split* into `slot_offers.py` (written offers, helpers the others share), `recruiter_calls.py` (recruiters' workflows) and `spoken_booking.py` (day, then time); no facade. *Run a message once* (`screener/run_once.py`): the claim, wait, run and record steps for the HTTP service and the voice endpoint; each keeps its own answers (409/429/500/503 and a 20 s wait; a spoken apology and 8 s). *Where a call stands* (`screener/voice_call.py`): not set up, ready, running, done after a call, done by text, plus when the page closes a finished call; the chat page only draws it. *Small ones:* `screener/launching.py` (port check, stopping processes), `screener/dates.py` (next weekday, minutes of the day), `sazon/areas.py` (the service areas leave `config.py`). *The call page's JavaScript* is `sazon/voice_call.js`, tested in Node with a fake SDK, microphone and page (skipped without Node). Two string assertions on the page's HTML changed with its shape; no other test assertion changed.
- **D49, D50, D59, D61, D67, D68: reviews and what they fixed.** Three review rounds, each fix with a test written first: opt-out never depends on the model; greetings and hesitation are not answers or strikes; injection hardening (data tags removed, 2000-character cap, model text rejected if it mentions pay, a hire or a time); versioned saves and race handling; erasure that really overwrites; login throttle; ids never reused.
