# DECISIONS

The settled design and technology choices for the Grupo Sazón screening agent, D1 to D69. Each entry states what is true **now**; a chain of revisions is merged into one entry and lists the numbers it replaces. Before acting against an entry, say so and confirm with the user. Add a short entry when a decision is made.

The full log, with every option considered and its pros and cons, is in git: `git show 4706568~1:DECISIONS.md` (it needs the merge commits of the pull requests; a squash merge would lose it).
All entries were decided by the user (2026-10-25 to 2026-10-27) unless marked *agent* (a fix or detail inside a decision the user had made).

## 1. How the conversation works

- **D5, D6, D27: flow.** The candidate may answer several things in one message; the agent asks only what is missing, in a friendly order (name, experience, availability, schedule, start date, city, license). A disqualifier that is already known (no license, outside the area) ends the chat at once instead of at the end, which protects recruiter time.
- **D40: confirm before rejecting.** A "no license" or out-of-area answer first gets one confirmation question; only a confirming reply ends the chat. Reason: the real model read "no tengo coche" as "no license", and one misread must never reject anyone.
- **D16, D17, D28, D41: unclear answers.** Every field is asked again with an explanation of what is needed. After 5 follow-ups the agent says it cannot continue until the candidate provides it, and repeats that: outcome `BLOCKED` (reason `BLOCKED_ON_<FIELD>`), cleared when the field is given. Model failures never count (D39). *(Replaces the cap of 3, the uncapped license and city, and NEEDS_REVIEW.)*
- **D19, D68: experience.** Years and platform are recorded and never disqualify. Zero years needs the candidate to say they have none (a separate `no_experience` flag); a plain "yes" is not a number. Common spellings of the known platforms are recognised; unknown ones are stored as "other" on purpose (no free text).
- **D26: field rules.** Name: two or more words of letters, hyphens, apostrophes. Start date: more than 12 weeks away is flagged, never rejected. Availability: "N days a week" below a full job is part-time; weekends needs weekends, Saturday or Sunday (D67).
- **D54: a changed answer** is confirmed ("Antes me dijiste X. ¿Lo cambio a Y?") and changed only on a yes, read in plain code.
- **D55: off-topic.** A fixed redirect and the same question; the third off-topic message in total (not in a row; hesitation like "mmm" does not count) ends the chat with a farewell about the messages, never about the person. Final until a recruiter acts.
- **D14, D50: asking for a person or about pay.** Not a strike. A fixed sentence says a recruiter reviews and will answer, then the next question; an event is logged. The agent does not hand over to a person itself.
- **D29, D56: stopping and coming back.** Stop wording ("stop", "para", "me retiro") is read in plain code and closes the chat as `WITHDRAWN`. While applications are open a candidate can come back: after silence or being blocked, after withdrawing ("continuar"), or after declining consent (a later "sí"). It stays closed after qualifying, after a rejection by the agent and after the off-topic exit. A dashboard switch closes applications for everyone.
- **D9, D52: disqualified path.** A polite close with a reason code ("I can't continue with the automated process; a recruiter can review your case"). Every such candidate is in the dashboard's review queue until a recruiter sets a status, so a human can overrule.
- **D7, D8, D36, D48: qualified path and call slots.** Recruiters add call times per country on the dashboard. A qualified candidate is offered the next three free times of their own country as a numbered list (clock time of that country, at least a few hours away). The pick is read in plain code; a booking is atomic, one per candidate, and a recruiter's cancellation queues a message. No free time, or "none", gives the 48-hour message.
- **D10, D35, D38, D58: silence.** One reminder after 24 hours; after 24 more, `STALLED`; any reply resets it. A reminder job (`python -m sazon.reminders`) queues the messages in an outbox for a real channel; it also covers qualified candidates who ignore the offer.
- **D11, D67: language.** The chat mirrors the candidate's language. It is decided in code from the words written (`screener/language.py`): a name, a place or a number changes nothing; the model's own guess is only used when no known word can check a long message.
- **D13: service areas.** A sample list (Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Ciudad de México, Guadalajara, Monterrey, with nearby towns) in one place; the client's real list is still needed.

## 2. What the agent says

- **D22, D45: the engine.** A pure-Python state machine (`Screening`) owns the fields, the decision and every message rule; the model only extracts fields and words messages. Reason: rules are unit-testable offline and candidate text can never change a decision.
- **D31, D39: extraction.** One JSON call per message, validated by a Pydantic model whose shape and prompt come from the client's spec. Candidate text is passed as delimited data. Unparseable output is retried once; a failure shows a fixed apology, is logged without candidate data and never costs the candidate a follow-up.
- **D15, D18, D30, D34, D37, D69: wording.** The client writes a persona (tone, length, form of address; Spanish uses "tú"). The model words the questions, follow-ups, place confirmations, change questions, rejection confirmations and the "cannot continue" message, one to two short sentences, given the recent conversation (only after consent, never on the turn that answers the consent question) as data. Every model message is checked (length, one question, language, no pay, job or time, and it must keep the facts it was given); a failed check or an unreachable model sends the fixed text. **Fixed word for word:** the AI disclosure and consent, every closing, the call offers and bookings, the reminders, the error line and all notices (a person, pay, change made, answer kept, off-topic). The model is told a notice was already said.
- **D44: consent is read in plain code.** A conservative Spanish and English word list reads the answer: short clear agreement is yes, a short explicit refusal closes the chat, anything else asks again. Nothing the candidate writes reaches the model before a yes.
- **D46, D66: the recruiter card.** Built by code from the validated data, never repeating the candidate's words or saying anything about the person. It is saved in Spanish with every turn (the audit snapshot) and rebuilt for the screen in the language the recruiter picked.

## 3. Privacy, consent and oversight

- **D12, D34, D51: disclosure and consent.** The first message is fixed, Spanish line then English line: it says the candidate is talking to an AI, that answers are processed by an external AI service and always reviewed by a recruiter, and asks consent to store them. Nothing is asked before a yes. The consent event stores a hash of the exact wording shown. Still a draft until the client's lawyers approve it.
- **D42: recruiter data is separate.** The agent owns outcome, reasons, flags, validated data, card and transcript. Recruiters own a status (`new`, `contacted`, `interview_scheduled`, `hired`, `rejected`, `unreachable`), an assignee and append-only notes, and may set any status on any candidate, so a human can overrule the agent. Every action is an audit event with its actor.
- **D53, D60: erasure and retention.** Every conversation and its data are saved and kept. The dashboard's erase action (with confirmation) overwrites a candidate's data, truncates the database's write-ahead log and leaves an anonymous line; it is for a candidate's request. There is no automatic deletion. How long to keep the rest is a question for the client's lawyers.
- **D57, D65: recruiter accounts.** One account per recruiter, scrypt-hashed in a git-ignored file (`python -m sazon.accounts add NAME`); five wrong passwords lock a name for ten minutes, kept in a file so a restart does not clear it. A prototype: a real deployment needs the company's single sign-on.
- **D71: optional CV upload (overturns the D20/D58 advice, at the user's request).** After consent the agent offers, once, an optional PDF upload together with the first question; typing an answer instead is the skip. The browser chat has an uploader with a fixed notice beside it (what is kept, who sees it, that it can be erased; uploading counts as agreeing to it), the terminal has `/cv <path>`. A PDF is checked by its first bytes and capped at 2 MB, 5 pages and 6000 characters; a scan, an encrypted or a broken file is kept for the recruiter and the questions go on. The model reads the text as data inside its own tag, with its own prompt that names only the fields the client allows from a CV (name, city, license, experience; never availability, schedule or start date), and everything it says about the conversation (consent, stop, language, off topic) is thrown away by code. A value counts when the client's own check accepts it. As chosen, clear values are taken without asking first, and the agent then reads them back so the candidate can correct them (a correction goes through the usual "change it?" question). A value that would end the screening (no license, a place outside the areas) or a near-miss place is never taken alone: it waits for the candidate through the usual confirmations, one at a time. The text is discarded after reading; the original PDF is kept in the same database transaction as the turn, erased with the candidate (bytes overwritten, log truncated), and offered to recruiters as a download, never drawn on the page. The card lists which answers came from the CV; events record size, a fingerprint and the fields, never the content. The CV never decides anything. The offer ends if the candidate withdraws, and an upload counts as a message (it brings back a stalled candidate). The upload event records a hash of the notice wording shown; the terminal prints the notice when the upload is offered. **A real channel must show the notice before it accepts a file.**
- **D72: the agent is called Sofía.** She names herself in the fixed disclosure ("soy Sofía, la asistente virtual (una IA) de Grupo Sazón") and in the answer to a request for a person, and the persona tells the model its name. The disclosure still says plainly that she is an AI. The consent hash changed with the wording, so earlier consent records point to the old text (the hash identifies which wording each candidate saw). The lawyers' list gets the new text.
- **D73: sharing the chat with the dashboard (adjusts D66).** In the browser chat a sidebar switch, off by default, sends new candidates to the recruiters' database so they appear in the dashboard; changing it starts a new candidate, so one chat never splits across two databases, and a warning shows while it is on. The dashboard now refreshes itself (D76). `python demo_launch.py` starts the chat (port 8501) and the dashboard (8502) together, checks the ports first and stops both on Ctrl+C.
- **D74: anyone can create a recruiter account on the login page (replaces the first design of D73, at the user's request; adjusts D57).** The login page always shows two tabs, sign in and create account (name, password twice), with no separate page when no accounts exist. Creating an account does not sign the person in; they sign in next. Every creation goes to the admin log (actor `signup`). **Risks accepted:** anyone who can reach the dashboard can create an account and read every candidate's personal data, so the dashboard must stay on your own computer until sign-up is restricted (the options considered: the first account free and later ones only from a signed-in recruiter, or a setup code from `.env`); the "already an account" message reveals which names exist; nobody can change a password; the accounts file is replaced atomically but not locked, so two accounts created in the same instant could lose one. A real deployment uses the company's single sign-on (D57).
- **D76: the two apps joined and polished; the dashboard at volume (decided with the user, 2026-10-28; adjusts D73).** Each app has a sidebar button to the other (`SAZON_CHAT_URL` and `SAZON_DASHBOARD_URL`, defaulting to the ports of `demo_launch.py`). The candidates page checks the database's change counter every 5 s: with no candidate open it reloads itself; with one open (a note may be half written) it only offers "refresh the list", so nothing a recruiter is doing is lost (a rule in `refresh_action`, tested; a timer cannot be run in a test, so check it once in a browser). D73's switch stays off by default. Both apps share one theme in `.streamlit/config.toml` (warm, one brand colour, no web fonts, so a candidate's browser contacts no font provider). Bugs fixed: the typing text and the failure message took the page's language on a candidate's first message (now the language of the message, by the engine's own rule), and the filter dropdowns said "Choose options" in English on a Spanish page. **At 5,000 fictional candidates** (a threshold set before measuring: a candidates-page rerun under 1 s) a rerun takes about 0.3 s (`tests/load/test_dashboard_volume.py`, run with `-m load`), reading every candidate 0.35 s (cached until something changes), the funnel's numbers 0.05 s; the one problem was the pick-a-candidate list holding all 5,000 entries, now one page plus the candidate a link names. Open sign-up stays as D74 (a local prototype; single sign-on is the real fix). *Agent* (details inside the decisions above, to confirm): the refresh is only offered, not applied, while a candidate is open; the warm brick-red theme; the heavy blue boxes for plain hints became captions. **Checked in a real browser** against a scratch database: a new candidate appears by itself, text typed in the search box survives, with a candidate open a half-written note survives and the offer works, and the page never shows the running indicator while idle. **Known limits:** Streamlit's own uploader text follows the browser's language, and a `?candidate=` link does not survive the login page. The Phase 1 document is cut to two pages (about 950 words, A4 at 10 pt, checked by printing it) and the reasons stay here; [HANDOVER.md](HANDOVER.md) lists what the client must provide and approve.
- **D77: the candidates page is one column (decided with the user, 2026-10-28; adjusts D66).** The filters sit on top, the table spans the full width below them, and the chosen candidate's details come under the table: the heading and badges, then two blocks side by side (the agent's summary, data and call; the recruiter's status, assignee, notes and erasure), then the conversation and audit tabs at full width. The table is as tall as its rows up to 10 rows (`table_height`), then scrolls inside itself. The 50-row pages stay (D62), so a page is quick with thousands of candidates. The "view candidate" list under the table is the keyboard alternative to clicking a row.
- **D78: the funnel covers the whole process, and a candidate's path (decided with the user, 2026-10-28; adjusts D58).** One pipeline from application to hire: started, consent, each answer, qualified, call booked (only for a client that books calls), then the recruiter's steps contacted, interview scheduled and hired (`screener/pipeline.py`, pure code, run through the second client too). The recruiter's steps are counted from the audit history and are cumulative: a candidate who was contacted and later rejected still counts as contacted, and one set straight to hired counts for the steps before it, so "contacted" can be higher than "call booked" (the chart says so). Every candidate has one of four marks: **in process**, **paused** (stalled or blocked: they can come back by writing), **out** (closed by the agent, withdrawn, ended off topic, rejected or unreachable) and **hired**. A recruiter's status overrules the agent's outcome (D42): contacted or an interview keeps a candidate in the process even if the agent closed the chat, and the agent steps they never finished show as "a recruiter took over"; `new` leaves it to the agent. A withdrawn candidate counts as out although "continuar" brings them back (D56): the mark is read live, so it flips back by itself. "Went through the whole process" is the number hired. Below the funnel a search and a short list pick one candidate (a full list would be thousands) and show each step as done, now, stopped here, a recruiter took over, or still ahead, and the reason for a candidate who is out. *Agent* (details inside the decisions above, to confirm): the "a recruiter took over" state for agent steps a recruiter skipped, the fourth number being the whole-process count (ever hired, like the chart) instead of a separate "hired" count, and lists of 20 to pick from.
- **D79: the call-times page is a calendar (decided with the user, 2026-10-28; adjusts D48).** A week grid per country (each country keeps its own clock; previous, today and next week), made with Streamlit's own elements: free times, booked times with the candidate's name, past times. Below it, "organise a call": a search and a short list of the candidates a call can be organised for, then a free time in the candidate's own country. Only candidates the agent qualified and no recruiter has closed (hired, rejected, unreachable) can be picked (`can_schedule`); a qualified candidate who ignored the offer is still qualified (D58), so they are the ones this is for. One booking per candidate stays (D48): a candidate who already has a call can be moved. Booking or moving is one atomic step (`move_booking`): the old time is freed, not cancelled, the new one booked, one audit line by `recruiter:NAME`. Then the candidate is told with a fixed text in their chat language (two new texts in the client's pack, to approve with the others), the chat's open offer of times is closed so a late "2" gets the booked time and the reminder job does not ask them to pick one. Adding a time, the list and cancelling are kept, under the grid. Declined: a third-party calendar component (JavaScript on a page of personal data, not testable here); overruling the agent to call a disqualified candidate; a second interview booking (needs a data model change). Streamlit cannot click a grid cell, so the time is picked from a list, or a new time is created in the candidate's country while booking (a time that cannot be booked is cancelled again). *Agent* (to confirm): separate fixed texts for "booked" and "moved" and their wording, a time someone else took meanwhile simply leaves the list instead of showing an error, a booking whose notice could not be saved stands and the recruiter is warned, and lists of 20 to pick from.
- **D43: errors.** An unexpected error never ends the chat or shows technical text; the error with its stack trace (never the candidate's messages) goes to `data/sazon.log` with an id.
- **D33: free-tier data.** Free Gemini prompts may be used by Google to improve its products: use fictional candidates only until a paid or EU-compliant tier is chosen.

## 4. Technology

- **D1, D66: channel.** Terminal chat first (`python -m sazon.chat`); a browser chat to try the agent (`apps/chat_app.py`) saves to a separate demo database and is local only. A real channel (WhatsApp or similar) comes later.
- **D2, D23, D33: model.** Gemini free tier through the OpenAI SDK against Gemini's compatible endpoint, a thin client behind an interface so tests use a fake. Model name from `LLM_MODEL` in `.env`.
- **D3: storage.** SQLite (versioned saves under a write lock, a change counter for the dashboard cache).
- **D4, D47, D66: recruiter tool.** A local Streamlit dashboard (`apps/dashboard.py`): candidate table beside a detail panel with row selection, colored status badges, metric cards, a funnel chart, a call-times page, the applications switch and the review queue. Every word is in a Spanish/English text pack with a language switch.
- **D75: HTTP service and load test (decided with the user, 2026-10-28).** `screener/service.py` is a FastAPI app factory (engine, no client named); `sazon/server.py` plugs Grupo Sazón in (`python -m sazon.server`; it refuses to start without `SAZON_API_KEY`). Calls: `POST /candidates` (returns the id, a secret token and the disclosure), `POST /candidates/{id}/messages` (`message_id` and `text`), `POST /candidates/{id}/cv` (multipart), `GET /candidates/{id}/outbox` and `POST .../outbox/ack` (the channel confirms what it sent, so nothing is lost if it crashes), `GET /health`. **Who may call:** a candidate's token opens only that candidate (only its SHA-256 is stored, erased with the candidate); the adapter's API key acts for any candidate and is needed to start one when set; a wrong token and an unknown id both answer 404. **One answer per message id:** the id is claimed in the store before any work; a resend gets the stored reply (a plain read, no write lock, no model call, no second save); a simultaneous copy waits for the first (409 after 20 s); a failed message releases its id; recording the reply is retried, and if it cannot be recorded the claim stays so a resend never runs the message again; a claim nobody finished is taken over after 5 minutes (a model call can take 20 s with two retries, and a turn has several). **Rate limit,** counted from the stored messages so it holds across workers and restarts: 20 a minute and 300 in total per candidate, answered 429 before the model call and before any save. **Uploads over 2 MB** (plus the multipart overhead) are answered 413 by a small layer in front of the app, by the declared size or by counting the bytes, before anyone parses the body, with or without a valid token. **Overload:** when the database's single writer cannot be reached in 10 s the answer is 503 with `Retry-After` (a resend of the same id is safe), never a 500; a candidate whose token could not be saved is erased again, so no empty candidate reaches the dashboard. **Unexpected errors** anywhere are logged with an id and answer `{error, error_id}` only (D43). Handlers are plain functions run in a pool of 200 threads (async was considered and declined: the model's rate limit and SQLite's single writer are the limits, not waiting threads; the model call happens before the save, so no lock is held while waiting for it). **Load test** (`pytest -m load`, a real uvicorn server, a fake model that sleeps 1 to 3 s and fails 3% of calls, about a fifth of candidates qualifying, messages sent twice at the same moment, resends after an apology). Assumed peak: 1000 applications a week, a fifth of them in the busiest 2 hours, about 10 messages each, which is about 0.3 messages a second with 8 to 17 chats open (an earlier figure of 2 a second was wrong). Results: with 200 candidates arriving over 150 s (about 7 requests a second, roughly 25 times that peak) p95 was 5.5 s with no busy answers and no errors; with all 200 arriving in the same instant 99 requests were told to retry, p95 was 19 s, one request waited 5 minutes (a reply that could not be recorded keeps its claim until it goes stale, so no message runs twice), and every candidate still ended correctly. Both runs check that every outcome is right, no call time is given twice and every answered message was applied exactly once. A 10-candidate version runs in every normal test run. **Measured limit:** SQLite on the development disk commits about 65 writes a second and each message makes about 3, so the service starts asking callers to retry beyond roughly 10 messages a second, about 35 times the assumed peak. Postgres, or `synchronous=NORMAL` (about 35% faster, a power cut may lose the last commits), is the next step if volume grows. The real model was not load-tested (the free tier allows 15 requests a minute).
- **D20, D58: bonuses.** Funnel analytics and the reminder job are built. CV upload is built (D71; it first was not recommended: new personal data, more AI Act duties). The voice agent (ElevenLabs) is not.
- **D21, D24, D25: process.** The Phase 1 design is Markdown in the repo; work is built in slices (domain core, extraction and chat, summary and dashboard) on branches from `main`.

## 5. Code structure

- **D62, D63, D64: engine and client.** `screener/` is the reusable engine and never imports or names a client; `sazon/` is Grupo Sazón. A client is a `ScreeningSpec` (fields, rules, wording, recruiter labels, persona), a `Pack` (fixed texts), a `Client` (service areas, optional call calendar, reminder clock) and its entry points. An incomplete spec or client fails when built. `tests/support/toy_spec.py` is a second client run through the real engine; `tests/engine/test_engine_boundary.py` fails if the engine leaks. Saved states, prompts, fixed texts and the consent hash are pinned by a golden test.
- **D63 differs from its review card:** screening state is a declared list of saved fields and a pure change-proposal function (not a state object with collaborators); the precedence of "what to say first" is a pure function, while the order of checks inside `apply` still lives in `Screening`. A `Store` interface was declined (one adapter).
- **D65: leftovers closed.** Funnel and key numbers counted in SQL; every stored time is naive local time; the login lockout is kept in a file.
- **D66: dashboard and chat app.** Text packs with a completeness check, per-language recruiter texts in the spec, the card rebuilt on screen, the redesign, and the browser chat with a live inspector.
- **D70: second architecture review (2026-10-27), all seven candidates built.** (1) `screener/conversation.py` is the one save cycle: it loads a candidate, lets the caller change it, saves, and applies the caller's policy on a lost race (raise, retry once, skip); the decision event is decided inside the store's transaction. (2) `screener/language.py` holds accent folding, the pick-with-the-client's-default rule, language detection and the pay, hire and time words; the word lists for yes, no, stop and call picks stay in `consent.py` and `slot_choice.py` (a third language still edits those). (3) `screener/recruiter_rules.py` defines NEEDS_REVIEW, TO_CONTACT, BLOCKED, STALLED and AGENT_CLOSED once, as Python and as SQL, with a test that they agree; `RecruiterView(client, pack, language)` gives every screen its labels, cards and rows. (4) `screener/directive.py` holds Directive and Kind, so deciding no longer depends on how a message is worded (it still takes the consent hash and the call-time labels from `phrasing`); notices are a tuple of checked names. (5) The engine says "area", not "city"; the names saved or sent to the model (`pending_city`, `out_of_area`, `confirms_city`) stay as wire names. (6) A table test of the order in which one message is processed (it found and fixed: a place refused and replaced in one message was still followed up). (7) `screener/dialog.py` shares starting a candidate and delivering queued messages. **Declined:** splitting `Screening`'s apply steps into another module (it would move the coupling, not remove it) and splitting reports out of `Store` (one adapter, one file).
- **D69: free wording** is described in section 2; it replaced the tests that said candidate text never reaches the wording prompt (it does now, only as data after consent, and a model that obeys an injection is not believed).

## 6. Reviews and what they fixed

- **D49, D50, D61:** three review rounds, each fix with a test written first. Notable: opt-out never depends on the model; greetings and hesitation are not answers or strikes; injection hardening (data tags removed, 2000-character cap, model text rejected if it mentions pay, a hire or a time); versioned saves and race handling; erasure that really overwrites; accounts safety and login throttle; ids never reused; one database for chat and dashboard.
- **D59:** erasure, return rules, versioned saves, accounts, the applications switch, review queue, funnel and reminder job were built and checked on the real model.
- **D67, D68:** found by trying the chat app: the language flip on a bare place name, "days a week" read as weekends, a plain "yes" saved as zero years, "uber" saved as "other", and screen text in the wrong language. Real-model tests (`pytest -m live`) pin them.

## 7. Open items

**For the client or its lawyers**
- The real service areas (D13).
- Legal review of the consent (now naming Sofía, D72) and farewell wording, and of promises nothing enforces: "a recruiter always reviews", "we reply within 48 hours" ("48 horas" is three literals in the pack).
- How long data may be kept (D60), including the uploaded CV PDFs; approved answers about pay.
- The CV (D71): the wording of the notice beside the uploader and of the read-back; that a PDF holds more than the screening fields (photo, age, address) which recruiters will see, a risk of bias; that the CV text goes to the external AI service (the free tier may train on it).
- Single sign-on instead of the prototype login; a paid model tier with a data-processing agreement (the free tier cannot serve about 1000 applications a week).

**Product**
- A candidate the agent rejected cannot reopen the chat themselves, and the dashboard has no "reopen" action.
- The reminder job must be started by someone and its messages wait for a real channel.
- The voice agent and the presentation (the format is not decided). CV upload is built (D71); a scan is not read (no OCR).
- The chat app has no login and must not be exposed on a network.
- The service has no real channel adapter yet (WhatsApp or similar) and no push of queued messages; the channel polls the outbox. Run it behind HTTPS, and keep the adapter's `SAZON_API_KEY` secret (D75).
- **Many qualified candidates at once compete for the same times** (the agent always offers the earliest three free ones): in a stress run with 70% qualifying, one candidate lost 20 picks in a row. At about a fifth qualifying it did not happen. If the client expects spikes of qualified candidates, offer a spread of times instead (changes D48).

**Technical**
- The candidate table still loads every candidate to filter and search it.
- A third language needs engine edits (word lists, day and month names, the language line of the extraction prompt) and the extraction prompt's filler examples are Spanish.
- The error log is not covered by any retention rule.
- The wording of model-written messages is less predictable: a legal review should read sample conversations, not only the fixed texts.
